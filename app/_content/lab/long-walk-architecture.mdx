I keep noticing that architecture decisions become easier after leaving the editor for a while.

Today I spent half an hour walking without trying to solve the problem. The issue was a small content-loading abstraction that had started accumulating exceptions.

The eventual solution was embarrassingly simple: the abstraction didn't need to know about every possible content type. It only needed to provide the filesystem-level operations, while the content-specific interpretation belonged one layer above.

This is probably one of those lessons that I'll have to relearn several times.

The annoying thing about software architecture is that the clean solution often looks obvious only after you've spent enough time making the messy one complicated.

---

There is probably a broader lesson here about abstraction itself.

An abstraction should remove a decision from the caller. If it merely moves the decision somewhere else, it may not be buying much.
