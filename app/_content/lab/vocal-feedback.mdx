I've been revisiting the idea behind Voccaria: useful vocal feedback probably shouldn't try to tell a singer whether a sound is "good."

That judgment is too contextual.

A more interesting tool might simply show what changed.

For example:

- spectral balance shifted upward
- harmonic energy became more stable
- fundamental frequency remained consistent
- overall amplitude stayed roughly the same

The singer can then decide whether that change corresponds to the sensation they were looking for.

That feels much more useful than inventing a single score for vocal quality.

## A question worth testing

Can objective measurements help someone reproduce a useful vocal sensation without turning practice into a dashboard?
